# ECHO Ø (Echo Null): 6-slide outline

Look: charcoal #1e1f21, VT323, off-white ink, dotted rules, the dithered flower on slide 1 only.

---

## 1. Your laptop can hear your hands.
- ECHO Ø turns any laptop into sonar.
- Visual: the dithered sound flower (Home page), title in VT323.
- Say: "No camera, no extra hardware. Just the speaker and mic you already have."

## 2. The problem
- Cameras watch you. Touchscreens and keyboards need hands on the device.
- People who can't touch or speak easily, people in the dark, and people who want privacy all go without.
- Gesture control normally means a camera or special hardware.

## 3. How the sonar works
- The speaker plays a 20 kHz tone that's too high to hear.
- A moving hand bounces it back slightly shifted (the Doppler effect): toward = higher, away = lower.
- The mic hears that shift, and a small model turns it into gestures: push, pull, wave, hold, open/close, undo.
- No camera, works in the dark, free on any laptop, runs in the browser.
- Visual: the live dithered waterfall with a hand streak.

## 4. Three apps
- **Lock:** a secret hand-sign password for the sites you choose.
- **Care:** hands-free help calls for hospital beds and ASL users.
- **Habit breaker:** catches repetitive moves (scratching, nail biting) and nudges you.

## 5. Live demo: the secret lock
1. Open a blocked site. It looks like a normal "This site can't be reached" page.
2. Do the 4 hand signs in the air. Only tiny dots show the progress, so nobody watching learns the code.
3. On the fourth sign the page opens.
- Backup: the recorded video if the room is too loud.

## 6. Honest limits, and the team
- A brand-new person gets about 1 in 2 gestures right today. It gets better after 5 minutes of calibration.
- PUSH and PULL still get confused at times. We're fixing that by retraining on more data.
